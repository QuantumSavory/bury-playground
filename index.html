THE IDEA BEHIND BURY

Keep a neighborhood together.

Each vertex is a qubit. Each edge represents a CZ operation in the graph-state description. A color assigns a qubit to a QPU; neighbors are allowed to share a color.

01 / COUNT

What does it cost to bury a vertex?

Count the uncolored vertices in its closed neighborhood: itself and its neighbors in the active graph. Initially, this weight is degree + 1.

02 / COLOR

Take the cheapest neighborhood.

Choose the lowest positive weight. If it is strictly smaller than the hamlet’s remaining space, color that neighborhood. Otherwise, assign one uncolored vertex.

03 / REPEAT

Fill, remove, and continue.

Update the weights. When a hamlet is full, remove it from the active graph and fill the next. The final hamlet gets the remaining vertices.

Algorithm conventions & what this demonstration measures

This app follows Algorithm 1 of the paper, including its strict w < r test. The Julia reference implementation uses w ≤ r, so partitions can differ. Ties and fallback vertices use the smallest vertex ID. Zero-cost vertices are already buried and need no visible step; weights are recomputed after every coloring.

Balanced target sizes differ by at most one. Custom sizes and continuation from fixed manual colors use the paper’s proposed extension: fill the hamlet with the most vacancies next. Other fixed colors are excluded from its active graph. This extension is not a recoloring optimizer. A completed manual assignment has no further BURY steps.

The score is F = Σi<j ν(G[Vi, Vj]): the sum of maximum bipartite matching sizes for all hamlet pairs. It is the Bell-pair cost of the paper’s vertex cover grafting (VCG) protocol, not a claim of the minimum cost across all possible protocols. Each pair is counted independently. Internal edges cost zero. Unassigned vertices are excluded from a provisional score.

BURY is a greedy construction and does not guarantee a globally optimal partition. Its score during construction may rise as more vertices receive colors. “Buried” refers to the remaining active graph; edges to earlier hamlets still count in the score. The QPU network is assumed to connect every pair of hamlets, regardless of the graph-state geometry. This playground covers static graph-state generation, not measurement scheduling.
